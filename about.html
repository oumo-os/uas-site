About UAS

A public scientific society advancing astronomy, space science and scientific discovery in Uganda.

Our Story

The Uganda Astronomical Society was founded in 2023 by Obwengye Cosmus as an amateur astronomy association — an organic response to public curiosity about the night sky, rather than a government, commercial or academic initiative. That origin remains part of the Society's institutional identity.

Within three years, UAS has grown into a national public-science community with an expanding membership base, established public astronomy programming, participation in Uganda's National World Space Week, and active engagement with the country's emerging space-science ecosystem. The September 2025 total lunar eclipse and the October World Space Week activities at Makerere University demonstrated public interest and national ecosystem participation. The May 2026 Stargazing Weekend at La Brise Resort confirmed genuine public demand for organised astronomy experiences — and exposed the Society's limitations in logistics, finance, equipment management, communications and volunteer coordination.

That growth has been carried by individual initiative and voluntary effort. The problem UAS must now solve is no longer public interest; it is organisational capacity. Membership growth, event demand and the Society's expanding role in national World Space Week programming have all outpaced its inherited, informal structure.

Our Vision

A scientifically curious, astronomy-literate and space-aware Uganda participating confidently in the understanding and peaceful exploration of the Universe.

Our Mission

To foster scientific curiosity, knowledge and participation in astronomy and the broader space sciences through observation, education, research, citizen science, public engagement and collaboration, while cultivating a vibrant Ugandan space culture.

Core Principles

The values that guide everything we do

Scientific Integrity

Evidence, observation and critical inquiry are foundational to everything we do.

Accessibility

Astronomy is open to everyone regardless of professional background or economic status.

Curiosity

Questions and wonder are legitimate starting points for scientific learning.

Participation

Members observe, contribute to citizen science, and take part in discovery — not merely consume it.

Inclusion

Pathways exist for students, amateurs, professionals, educators and institutions alike.

African Context

Ugandan and African astronomical knowledge, history and cultural relationships with the sky are treated with serious attention.

Collaboration

UAS strengthens the ecosystem by connecting existing capability rather than duplicating it.

Peaceful Exploration

The Society supports the peaceful advancement and application of space science.

What UAS Is and Is Not

UAS Is

A public scientific gateway through which Ugandans encounter astronomy, develop scientific curiosity, and find pathways into the broader space sciences, engineering and technology — anchored in astronomy as its core mandate.

UAS Is Not

  • Uganda's national space agency
  • A government regulator
  • An aerospace engineering or rocket-development company
  • A commercial satellite operator
  • A substitute for specialised academic or professional institutions

UAS plays a complementary role within the national ecosystem.

Our Journey

Five stages of institutional development

Stage I — 2023
Formation: Amateur astronomy association founded by Obwengye Cosmus. An organic response to public curiosity about the night sky.
Stage II — 2025
Public Discovery: The September total lunar eclipse and October World Space Week activities at Makerere University demonstrated public interest and national ecosystem participation.
Stage III — Early 2026
Community Expansion: Renewed social-media engagement produced a significant influx of members into the Society's WhatsApp community.
Stage IV — May 2026
Proof of Concept: The La Brise Resort Stargazing Weekend confirmed genuine public demand — and exposed limitations in logistics, finance, equipment and volunteer coordination.
Stage V — 2026 onward
Institutionalisation: UAS builds identity, governance, membership, programmes, partnerships and institutional capacity. This is the stage we are in now.

Governance

Four levels of institutional accountability

UAS operates through four institutional levels: Governance → Administration → Programmes → Activities. General Membership elects a Governing Executive, which directs Executive Officers, who coordinate Programme Leads, who mobilise Working Groups and Volunteers for specific activities.
